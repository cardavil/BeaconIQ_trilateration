Exploratory Data Analysis — live
What these numbers mean (plain language)
  • Sessions — each recording you made (stand or walk while the app logs for a while).
  • Readings — each time the phone saw a beacon at an instant. A session holds hundreds.
  • RSSI mean (dBm) — how strong the signal arrives. Closer to 0 = stronger/nearer; more negative = farther/weaker.
  • RSSI σ (dBm) — how much the signal trembles even when you stand still. ~9 dB is a lot: it is the root of why positioning is hard.
  • Kalman σ↓ — the filter reduces the distance jitter by this much. It helps, but does not fix the base noise.
  • Proximity acc. — of the readings with a ground-truth zone, how often the model got the zone right.
  • Prox. eval reads — the subset where you annotated "I was in this zone". It is the only data that can grade the model.
  • iBeacon hit-rate — of all the Bluetooth the phone picks up, the % that is our beacons. The rest is ambient noise (earbuds, watches, TVs…).
Glossary (one line each)
  • RSSI (dBm): signal strength. Closer to 0 = stronger. Always negative.
  • Kalman: a filter that smooths a noisy measurement by averaging intelligently.
  • Zone: the nearest beacon/spot (the proximity model does not use coordinates).
  • Confidence: how sure the model is about the chosen zone (0 to 1).
  • Coverage: % of readings kept when demanding a given confidence.
  • Hit rate: % of captured Bluetooth that is actually our beacons.
  • TX power: the expected RSSI at 1 m — the anchor of the distance formula.
  • Path-loss N: how fast the signal decays with distance (2 = free space; higher indoors).
